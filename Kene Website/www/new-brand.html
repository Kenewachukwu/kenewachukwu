Project Background

Mixtape’s account-creation flow had a positioning problem baked into its logic: onboarding assumed brands with an internal marketing team evaluating a B2B tool, when most signups were actually solo creators or small DTC brands who just wanted their footage turned into ads. That mismatch — flagged internally as CR-002, the “audience mis-wire” — showed up as friction days before anyone saw a result. This flow rebuilds account creation around a B2C default, and gets a first, tangible payoff in front of the user before the session ends.

The ad brought them here, so the ad stays in the room
Someone arriving from a Storyteller ad sees a headline about turning footage into stories; someone from a KeyMO ad sees one about finding which scenes are winning. The landing page keeps whichever context brought the visitor in, and the URL they drop pre-fills the account flow that follows — so the thing that convinced them to click never has to be explained twice.
Account first, nothing else
Sign-up asks for exactly three things — email, password, a Terms checkbox — plus one-tap Apple and Google options. A testimonial sits in the dark rail beside the form, doing the reassurance work so the form itself doesn’t have to.
Brand basics before anything gets typed
Step two asks for a brand name and a website — nothing else. The website is what unlocks the next step: everything past this point gets pre-filled from it instead of typed by hand.
Confirm, don’t fill in — and default to B2C
This is where CR-002 actually gets fixed. Audience, category, offering, commerce model and country all arrive pre-filled by reading the brand’s own site, with Audience defaulting to B2C instead of the B2B assumption the old flow carried. The user’s job changes from filling out a form to confirming five guesses — most of which are already right.
One product is enough to start
Rather than asking a new brand to set up a full catalog on day one, step four asks for a single product URL. That’s enough to generate a first story — the rest of the catalog can come later, once the tool has already proven itself once.
Let people choose their own first win
Immediately after signup we ask what the user wants to do first — make new ads (Storyteller) or measure the ads they’re already running (KeyMO) — instead of forcing everyone down one path. Someone with a campaign already live cares about a completely different kind of first value than someone starting from zero footage, and the fork respects that.
Ship a rough cut before asking for anything else
The payoff for all of the above: a first story, auto-assembled from the brand’s own uploaded footage, broken into a Hook / Problem / Proof / Transformation / Payoff structure with real timestamps and real creators attached to each beat. It’s explicitly a rough cut — no captions or music yet — but it’s real, it’s theirs, and it happens in the same session they signed up in.
